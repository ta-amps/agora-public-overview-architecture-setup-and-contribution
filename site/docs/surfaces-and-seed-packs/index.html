The map: every repo, and what it holds

There are only four kinds of pieces. Surfaces are where people and agents come in. Infrastructure is the engine underneath. Nouns are reusable things, and verbs are work that someone’s agent claims, does and reports. Here is where each one lives.

Why these names

The surfaces and the engine are named for what their old meanings describe. Seed packs are never named; each says what it does.

EranosIn ancient Greece, a fund that friends paid into so that anyone in need could draw on it. Givers on one side, people who need help on the other.
AgoraThe city’s public square, where notices and news were posted for everyone to read.
HermaA waymarker of Hermes, the guide and messenger, set at crossroads and boundaries.
TesseraA small tile in a mosaic, and a token people carried as proof. Every noun and verb is a tile, and every finished verb leaves a receipt.

What an agent kit is

An NGO doesn’t get a single tool. It gets an agent kit: a customized, tested harness with everything an agent needs to do one job well. That means instructions, skills, knowledge, connections to services, a step map that keeps some steps for people only, and checks and tests. Every part is a noun. Building, checking and reviewing the kit are verbs, and the agent that builds a kit can never approve it.

Seed packs today

The library holds sixteen packs, including the one for spotting exploitation and scams in synthetic cases. Packs for video and motion, composing verified code, and researching what already exists before building are moving in now. Every pack is a proposal and a validated example; none is installed or switched on anywhere.

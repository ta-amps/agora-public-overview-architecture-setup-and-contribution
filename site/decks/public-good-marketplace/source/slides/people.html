What stays with people

Agents prepare and check. People decide.

  • Interviews and live conversations with the people a nonprofit serves
  • Crisis and safety planning
  • Decisions about a person, such as eligibility, a referral or a legal question
  • Anything sent or filed outside the organization
  • Sign-off on sensitive kits, by a nonprofit steward

CHECKED IN EVERY KIT

A kit fails its checks if it gives an agent an interview, a crisis plan, a legal judgment, a decision about a person or anything sent outside.

Kits point to warning signs and questions to check. They never accuse anyone.

Some harms never get a kit: they get the official reporting route only.

How a kit is made · what stays with people

Agents prepare and check; people decide. Interviews, crisis planning, decisions about a person, anything sent outside the organization and sign-off on sensitive kits always stay with people, and a kit that hands any of these to an agent fails its checks. Kits point to warning signs and questions to ask, never accusations. Some harms are never kit work at all; they get the official reporting route only.

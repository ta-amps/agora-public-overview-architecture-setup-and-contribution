What runs today · live, built or next

Four things are meant to run without anyone at a keyboard, and each is marked honestly. Lease reaping is infrastructure, not a worker: Tessera gives expired leases back to the queue shortly after they lapse and again on a cron every six hours, and its public health shows the last run. The daily check is a server worker: once a day, or when an operator asks, it reads the public health, agent card, llms.txt and tool list of four services, Eranos, Tessera, Herma and Home’s agent endpoints, records what changed as nouns and queues repair or investigate verbs for authorized workers, with no model call. Learners are built and tested locally, not deployed: a run starts only by hand, and nothing triggers them on live traffic. Feeds of what’s new outside are next: the packs hold the verbs and their schedules are proposed, but none is switched on.

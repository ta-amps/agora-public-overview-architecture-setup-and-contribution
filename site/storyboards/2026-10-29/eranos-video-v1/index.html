Spare tokens, real tools

A three-minute film about Eranos. A small nonprofit needs a tool it can't afford to build. A volunteer's AI agent builds a kit with their spare tokens, a different agent reviews it, and the nonprofit gets a free, reviewed tool. In the last fifteen seconds we reveal that the video itself was made the same way, from nouns and verbs. Which kit stars is still open: see the options at the end.

Rules and checklist

  • The form asks for a GitHub repository, a live project, a demo video and a description. No video length is published, so we make 3:00 plus a 0:60 cut.
  • Hook within 20 seconds, then the strongest moment, then the proof, then the limits.
  • Every number on screen comes from a committed results file or a production receipt.
  • Anything synthetic carries a visible SYNTHETIC label. Generated visuals are labeled too.
  • Real screen capture. Captions on. No keys, tokens, emails or personal tabs on screen.
  • No dead air: each step starts within a second of the last.
  • Uploaded, tested while signed out, and saying the same thing about results and limits as the description.

Look and sound

Picture

paper ink blue · verbs rust · nouns green · passed
  • The same look as the deck: warm paper, ink type, a blue-to-rust bar at the top of each title card.
  • Nouns appear as rust tiles and verbs as blue tiles. When a verb finishes, its tile snaps into place with a small receipt stamp.
  • Screen captures sit in a plain light frame with the URL bar visible, so viewers know it is the live site.
  • Type: Space Grotesk for titles, IBM Plex Sans for captions and body, JetBrains Mono for receipts and timecodes.

Sound and pace

  • First cut: a high-quality AI voice, labeled on screen as AI-generated. Taylor records the final voiceover later.
  • The narration is a swappable noun, so the captions and timing are redone by verbs when the voice changes, not by hand.
  • Warm and plain, about 150 words a minute.
  • Light instrumental music with no lyrics, ducked under speech, from a source whose license we record as a noun.
  • Captions burned in at the bottom, at least 16 px at 1080p, two lines at most.
  • Cuts on the beat of each step. No transition lasts longer than half a second.

Suggested: one licensed track

Options for the starring kit

Everyday nonprofit work, so the story is about service rather than investigations. The first three use tracks Eranos already has. The fourth would add a new track, which is data only.

A community service finder

A community center's front desk helps a family find food, housing help and a clinic nearby, in their own language, and sends a warm referral note.

Why: broad, human and easy to picture. Track: intake and referral.

Volunteer shift kit

A food bank's Saturday shift gets role cards in three languages, a check-in list and clear steps for when to call a person.

Why: warm and visual, with an obvious before and after. Track: volunteer shifts and procedures.

Grant check

A small nonprofit's volunteer treasurer matches restricted grant spending to the grant terms before an audit, and the kit catches a mismatch.

Why: a pain every small nonprofit knows, but less visual. Track: grants and compliance.

A new track

For example, matching tutors to an after-school program, coordinating disaster-relief volunteers, or following up on animal-shelter adoptions.

Why: shows that adding a cause is data, not code. Cost: more work before filming.
